\chapter{Results and ablations}
\label{cha:ablations}

The benchmark in Chapter~\ref{cha:results} establishes the forecasting performance of the proposed models. The ablations examine what supports that performance: whether the fitted models use their visual inputs, which properties of those inputs matter, and how the fusion interface affects transfer to unseen plants. Unless stated otherwise, results use the UK cross-plant test, the self-attention temporal mixer, and seeds 42, 43, and 44. Interpretation follows the size and direction of the observed changes, their consistency across matched seeds, and the variables changed by each intervention. Small changes are informative when they narrow the explanation of a larger gain; losses reveal where an additional design choice fails to improve the tested configuration.

The main result is that spatial-token fusion improves point forecasts across most held-out plants and depends on timely, site-matched imagery. The controls refine this finding. Fractional token placement has little observed effect, mean pooling improves skill while retaining spatially distinct cells, and freezing the numerical encoder largely preserves point performance at a calibration cost. The multi-anchor experiments extend the same visual interface to older observations and expose a trade-off between temporal coverage and the payload available at each anchor. Together, these comparisons provide an empirical account of the fusion design, with the scope of each conclusion tied to the intervention that produced it.

Controls preserve the plant split, seed-matched numerical warm start, and scoring rules unless a difference is stated.

The anchor-placement check asks how much the forecast depends on the precise positions assigned to visual tokens. Moving the same 98 tokens to one shared integer position changes mean skill by $-0.02$ percentage points and ramp NMAE by $+0.0004$ across three paired seeds. Both differences change direction across seeds. The fitted interface therefore tolerates this positional simplification with little observed change in point performance. This narrows the explanation of the spatial-token gain: fractional placement is not supported as its main source. The check preserves the visual payload and tests one placement change, so its conclusion concerns positional precision within this interface.

Under paired-novelty selection, multi-anchor skill reaches 54.16 percent, a 0.07 percentage point increase over the default selector. Because changing the trajectory selection rule leaves the gap to spatial-token fusion (55.10 percent) largely unchanged, the performance difference arises from token budget dilution and multi-day dispersion across anchors.

Image timing produces a much larger response. Shifting the clip six hours into the past raises ramp NMAE by between 0.0156 and 0.0182 across three seeds and lowers skill by about 13.5 percentage points. The stale clip performs worse than disabling vision. Supplying an image can therefore hurt the fitted model when that image describes an earlier state. The consistent degradation shows that the model's use of vision depends on temporal relevance and supports matching the clip to the forecast origin.

Spatial correspondence matters as well. Substituting another plant's contemporaneous clip raises ramp NMAE by between 0.0163 and 0.0189 across three seeds and lowers skill from about 55 to 37 percent. Every seed degrades. Contemporaneous imagery from elsewhere cannot substitute for the site's own clip in the fitted model. Together, the timing and wrong-plant controls support content grounding: predictions respond to whether the supplied visual evidence matches the place and time being forecast. These substitutions identify the importance of that correspondence without identifying the individual visual features responsible.

At the fixed 98-token budget, random selection raises ramp NMAE by 0.0039 and lowers skill by 1.52 percentage points across two paired seeds. Vision-off scores stay unchanged. Holding the count fixed makes the selected payload relevant to the result: equal-length visual inputs do not give equal performance. Novelty-selected tokens better serve the model trained with that selector. Because the intervention changes selection only at inference, the observed loss includes any mismatch with the training distribution; it supports retaining the trained selection rule without establishing its superiority under separate retraining.

Permuting V-JEPA's cached temporal latents after visual encoding changes ramp NMAE by no more than 0.00030 in any seed. The small response localizes the observed sensitivity: the downstream forecast depends little on this ordering of the already encoded latents. V-JEPA may have mixed motion information within each latent before the permutation, so this result concerns the fusion stage's access to latent order. Frame order before encoding remains unchanged. The check helps distinguish reliance on the encoded visual content from reliance on its explicit downstream temporal arrangement.

The token-budget sweep shows that increasing the available visual payload does not automatically improve a fitted model. Evaluating without pruning to the 98-token budget raises ramp NMAE by 0.0102 across three seeds. Keep settings of 0.3 and 0.7 raise it by 0.0022 and 0.0069. Error is lowest near the count used during training. These results support preserving the trained token budget at inference and show that the interface is sensitive to sequence length. Since the alternative settings are evaluated without retraining, 98 tokens is the best tested inference setting for these weights, with no claim of a universal budget optimum.

The spatial-resolution probe finds higher ramp $R^2$ with a mean-pooled 4$\times$4 grid than with 7$\times$7 at 30, 60, and 120 minutes. A coarser representation can therefore retain useful predictive information and outperform the finer grid under this probe. This result weakens the assumption that additional spatial cells necessarily improve the representation and motivates considering compression as a design variable. Both probe grids use mean pooling, whereas the deployed arm uses pixel-shuffle concatenation, so the comparison ranks the probe representations and leaves the corresponding end-to-end grid choice unresolved.

The end-to-end pooling check provides a more direct test of sub-patch detail. With the number and positions of selected cells fixed, replacing pixel-shuffle concatenation with mean pooling raises three-seed skill from 55.10 to 55.64 percent. The skill gain appears in every paired seed. Ramp NMAE falls from 0.1440 to 0.1435, and 80 percent coverage rises from 0.731 to 0.753. The larger change in skill and smaller change in ramp error show that the representation choice affects the reported metrics to different degrees. Spatially distinct cells remain available under mean pooling, so the result supports a compressed spatial payload and revises the motivation for preserving sub-patch detail. The original spatial-token gain does not require that detail in this comparison.

Freezing the adapted numerical encoder's blocks during visual-arm training yields three-seed skill of 55.29 percent and ramp NMAE of 0.1441, close to spatial-token fusion's 55.10 percent and 0.1440. Point forecasting therefore remains effective when visual training leaves those blocks fixed. The uncertainty measures reveal a separate trade-off: coverage falls from 0.731 to 0.710, while quantile ECE rises from 0.0359 to 0.0444. Allowing encoder adaptation has more apparent value for calibration than for the point scores in this check. The intervention changes the trainability of the blocks together and does not attribute the calibration difference to a particular block.

Spatial-token fusion has the highest skill, 55.10 percent, and the lowest ramp NMAE, 0.1440, among the main fusion configurations in Table~\ref{tab:ablation-ladder}. Relative to pooled-image fusion, skill increases by 2.52 percentage points and ramp NMAE falls by 0.0047. Pooled-image fusion produces a smaller change from the numerical baseline: skill rises from 52.30 to 52.58 percent and ramp NMAE falls from 0.1506 to 0.1487. The comparison favors the spatial-token configuration for point forecasting on unseen UK plants. Since fusion path and training recipe differ between arms, the controls are needed to determine which parts of this advantage involve the visual input.

The vision-off columns test image use within each fitted model by holding its weights fixed. Disabling vision in pooled-image fusion raises all-daylight NMAE by 0.0008, while the mean ramp change rounds to 0.0000 and has no consistent direction across the three seeds. Its modest point-score improvement thus gives limited evidence of a visual contribution to ramp forecasting. In spatial-token fusion, the same intervention raises all-daylight NMAE by 0.0047 and ramp NMAE by 0.0063, with degradation consistent across seeds. This connects the better point forecasts to reliance on the visual pathway. The stale-image and wrong-plant controls further show that this reliance is sensitive to the supplied content. The larger absolute ramp change should be read alongside the different error baselines for ramp and all-daylight targets; it alone does not establish a concentration of visual benefit on ramps.

Multi-anchor fusion scores 54.09 percent skill and 0.1463 ramp NMAE. It improves on the numerical baseline, while remaining below spatial-token fusion on both point measures. Its mean vision-off increases of 0.0031 NMAE and 0.0045 ramp NMAE show that the model uses images within the distributed layout. Extending visual context across daily anchors therefore retains a visual contribution, but does not improve the UK point scores over the recent single-anchor configuration. This comparison jointly changes anchor count, daily spacing, latent depth, and per-anchor token budget. It measures the consequence of that combined design and cannot assign the gap to older observations alone.

The full payload feasibility run retains four latent slices and 98 tokens at each of five anchors. It reaches 54.49 percent skill and 0.1458 ramp NMAE, with vision-off increases of 0.0040 and 0.0041. This run demonstrates that a larger multi-anchor payload can be trained and used by the forecast. Its 533-position sequence is substantially longer than the 141-position single-anchor interface. One completed seed and a smaller eligible target set make its point-score ranking descriptive. The experiment establishes feasibility and visual reliance for this larger interface; the recorded results do not establish that restoring payload closes the gap to spatial-token fusion.

The matched seeds provide a check on whether the configuration rankings depend on a particular numerical warm start. Visual arms with the same seed begin from the same adapted numerical checkpoint, although their subsequent training trajectories differ. Point metrics show limited variation within each arm across the three recorded runs.

Spatial-token fusion improves both skill and ramp NMAE over the numerical baseline in every paired seed. Multi-anchor has lower skill and higher ramp NMAE than spatial-token fusion in all three pairs. These rankings persist across the recorded initializations and agree with the per-plant and lead-time patterns. Three runs give a bounded view of training variability, but the agreement supports the observed configuration preference. Explaining the multi-anchor loss still requires separating anchor count, temporal depth, token allocation, and daily spacing.

The probabilistic measures assess different properties. CRPS summarizes quantile forecast error across the predicted distribution. Coverage counts how often observations fall between the predicted 10th and 90th percentiles; that interval should contain about 80 percent of observations. Quantile ECE measures departures between each quantile's stated probability and its observed frequency.

Calibration varies more clearly across the recorded configurations. Spatial-token coverage is below the nominal 80 percent in every seed. Its quantile ECE exceeds the numerical baseline's in every seed. Multi-anchor coverage is also below nominal in all three runs. Its ECE is higher than spatial-token fusion's in each matched seed. Spatial-token fusion has lower CRPS than the numerical baseline in each recorded run, with three-seed means of 0.0544 and 0.0574, respectively.

The lower CRPS shows that spatial-token fusion improves the reported distributional error as well as the point scores, while coverage and ECE identify a calibration cost. These measures capture different properties of the forecast and should inform different modeling decisions. The freezing control is relevant here: it leaves point scores close to the original arm while worsening calibration further. Interval widths and individual quantile predictions are unavailable for this analysis, so the undercoverage cannot be attributed specifically to narrow intervals, displaced intervals, or a combination of the two.

The full payload seed has higher skill and coverage than multi-anchor seed 42, with identical ramp NMAE when rounded to four decimals. It also scores fewer forecast targets. This is evidence from one larger multi-anchor run, so the apparent improvement cannot establish a repeatable payload effect. Pooled-image fusion illustrates a different form of run variation: coverage is below nominal in two seeds and slightly above it in the third, while point scores remain near the numerical baseline.

Taken together, the ablations support spatial-token fusion as the strongest tested main configuration for UK cross-plant point forecasting. Its improvement is accompanied by image reliance, sensitivity to timing and site correspondence, and gains distributed across held-out plants and forecast leads. The smaller-effect controls refine the design argument: precise fractional placement contributes little in the tested intervention, mean pooling preserves the useful spatial interface while improving skill, and encoder adaptation has a clearer benefit for calibration than for point prediction. The multi-anchor results document useful visual forecasts with a longer history, alongside a consistent UK point-score loss relative to the recent single-anchor arm. These findings establish which configurations worked and which simplifications remained effective, while keeping the causal interpretation at the level resolved by the completed experiments.
